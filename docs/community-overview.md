# Gridiron Dynasty Engine: NCAA 14 Dynasty, Expanded

NCAA 14 is still the game on the field. Gridiron Dynasty Engine (GDE) is the companion that makes the dynasty around it deeper, easier to manage, and safer to carry forward.

GDE is built for NCAA 14 and College Football Revamped players who enjoy the long view: recruiting classes, roster turnover, staff movement, postseason stakes, program identity, and the stories that accumulate across years. It does not replace NCAA. You still play the games, make the calls, and save your dynasty in NCAA. GDE adds a modern management layer around that experience.

## A modern dynasty layer without losing the original game

The portal, recruiting, roster planning, and staff decisions in a long dynasty can become hard to track with the tools available inside an older game. GDE gives those decisions context. It helps identify roster needs, explain position health, examine transfer possibilities, track program momentum, and preserve the history behind each season.

The first-season Initial Portal uses a temporary staging pool to help organize the opening roster transition without creating permanent extra slots. After Year 1, transfer work follows the normal offseason cycle: reconcile the NCAA-native roster, sequence approved moves, preserve class and redshirt context, and keep source and destination rosters coherent.

Development Reserve adds a deliberate option for a player who needs a season outside the active NCAA roster. It is bounded: an active roster can have up to 70 players, Reserve can hold up to 10, and your controlled population remains capped at 80. A Reserve stay lasts one season and must resolve through activation, transfer, or cut the following offseason. It is designed as a real roster decision, not a way to stockpile unlimited talent.

## Better decisions, not automatic decisions

GDE's planning tools are intentionally human-facing. The Position Change Lab looks beyond a flat overall-rating change: it considers target-position fit, athletic and skill context, likely role, team need, and career runway. The Offseason Decision Center asks the useful question: *what decisions do I need to make?* It brings Active, Reserve, staging, position health, recruiting needs, and candidate actions together in one place.

The Dynasty What-If Simulator asks the complementary question: *what happens if I make these decisions?* Build a multi-action scenario with transfers, cuts, Reserve moves, additions, or credible position changes, then compare before and after. The simulation never modifies NCAA or authoritative GDE state. Saved scenarios can become stale when the real roster changes, making the boundary between plan and reality clear.

## More reasons for every season to matter

GDE connects the regular season to the larger program story. Program Momentum and Portal Attractiveness surface signals around results, opportunity, roster fit, and coaching context. CFP and postseason tools help organize the championship run. A national title unlocks one following-season Championship Reward—such as a qualifying Championship Commitment, Elite Scouting, Development Edge, or Retention Edge—while Program Momentum remains its own automatic outcome.

Staff, captains, recruiting, and player development belong to the same long-term picture. The goal is not to overwrite NCAA's identity; it is to give dynasty players better information and supported choices at the moments where a program changes direction.

## Built around safe save integration

GDE treats an NCAA save as important. Analysis tools are read-only. When a supported workflow can change NCAA-native data, GDE requires a closed save, shows a preview, takes a managed backup, validates the proposed change, rereads the result, and keeps an audit/rollback path. The user then reloads NCAA, verifies the result, and saves normally.

GDE also keeps its own durable local history. Its embedded database supports multiple dynasties, automatic backups, portable export/import, explicit schema migration, and fail-closed recovery. Your data is stored under your Windows user profile rather than temporary files or the installed application folder.

That combination is the point of V1: a deeper NCAA 14 dynasty experience that respects the game, respects the save, and leaves the final decisions with the player.
